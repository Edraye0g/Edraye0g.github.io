

The nonlinear nature and synergetics of complex system such as global climate system, bio-diversity ecosystems represents a scientific hurdle. The complex system mostly approached by two different axes. The empirical standing of high volume and high velocity big data and against it is the computational infrastructure to process system. Yet, with the advancement of machine learning, deep neural networks and data analytics, complexity science makes zero sense when it faces the *complexity* of predictive modeling. How can you imply predictive modeling when the system is nonlinear as well as state-dependent.

The traditional modeling paradigms faces huge backlash while dealing with the rigorous architecture of any complex system. *Conventional Bottom-ups, reductionist, and agent-based modeling* uses micro-scale approach to illustrate the heavy macro-scale phenomena. But, in complexity science, micro scale phenomena is rarely observable. The bottom-up reductionist models accumulate unvalidated and unobservable assumptions for micro scale parameters. This introduces dynamic brittleness where models fit historical data but failed to comply with out-of-distribution regime shifts.


> Here comes the MaxEnt which establishes a robust baseline of least-biased inference by explicitly constraining predictions to known information without parameter over-fitting.

## Historical Foundation of Inference

- Jacob Bernoulli in his *Principle of Insufficient Reasoning* Stated that in the absence of defining information, all possible outcomes should be treated as equally likely. 
- In the mid 18th century, Thomas Simpson, Thomas Bayes and Abraham de Mouvre, independently constructed the mathematical background of probabilistic inference, expanding the Bernoulli framework. 
- Pierre-Simon Laplace took it further with inverse probability. He founded the modern statistical framework of reasoning under uncertainty. 
- Claude Shannon developed mathematical *Communication theory and Information Entropy* which is the cornerstone of modern Information theory. 
- At 1957, Edwin T. Jaynes connected Shannon's information entropy with Bernoulli and Laplace's principle of insufficient reasoning into the Maximum Entropy Principle. 
- At 1984, John Skilling said that MaxEnt is the systemic approach to evaluate the undetermined scientific problem, enabling predictions for incompletely characterized complex systems. 


To address the uncertainty and the predictive modeling of complex system, there are four premises that defines the expansion or evolution of certain infrastructure. The scientific progress is an iterative progress and it rests on the shoulder of **prior knowledge**. The complex system is incomplete and to expand the knowledge, the duty is to go through the **probabilistic expression**. As the complex system cannot be taken as individual micro components, prior knowledge must be codified into macro-scale expectation constraints to act upon the probabilistic distributions. And, lastly, the whole scenario must be completely least biased; meaning they must maximize the remaining uncertainty and must not contain any out-of-distribution assumption and create overfitting. This results overcoming macro-scale constraints. 

## Maximum Entropy
MaxEnt respects constraints of macro scale phenomena while capturing the uncertainty. It accounts both of the parameters. The Jaynes objective value for MaxEnt formalism lies in providing unbiased and non-arbitrary procedure for deriving micro scale probability distributions from undetermined prior information. Rather than introducing arbitrary functional forms or invalidated microscale parameters, MaxEnt accepts available macro-level knowledge as formal constraints, maximizing an information-theoretic objective function to derive unique probability distribution. 

![](/assests/maxent2.png)

> *The state produced by MaxEnt represents the honest representation of knowledge: it assumes complete randomness/uniformity beyond the boundaries imposed by what you actually know.*

In simpler terms, predicting micro-level components, like gas molecules, individuals and economic decision making, behave based only on macro level facts like temperature, total population or total GDP. There are infinitely many possible outcomes. MaxEnt only selects the flattest, most spread-out distribution possible. 

> *Constraints are what injects knowledge into the problems. What constraints do is treat any possibilities equally. These do not invent any other knowledge other than the one provided.*
> A constraint based agent can be overhauled by knowledge. Excess knowledge can make the scene tricky. Agent can get confused and the model can fail. The main pinning down substance of MaxEnt is information. Scientists argue that in biological system, intelligence depends on what is ignored than on what is perceived. 

![](/assests/maxent3.png)

But how the information overhauling is dealt? Here comes the constraints selection mechanism. The task shifts from inference to selection which preceeds by justification and connection. Jaynes principle assumes an observer who knows what information is relevant. But real world is completely different. It transits from "What distribution to choose" to "What should be the condition to rely on." This is why the system with information first **Filters and evaluates Reliability.** Then the constraints is determined and MaxEnt is applied. This looks like a **cognitive overload,** not lack of information but what information is important.

### What happens when a MaxEnt prediction fails empirical testing?
Empirical failure in MaxEnt serves as a diagnostic tool for scientific discovery. Because MaxEnt produces the flattest probability distribution compatible with specified constraints, systematic discrepancies between predictions and empirical observations demonstrate that the assumed constraint set is incomplete. This diagnostic failure is analogous to physical thermodynamics, where ideal gas law failures revealed Van der Waals molecular forces. In macroecology, log-series abundance discrepancies led directly to the $1/n^{r+1}$ multi-resource niche expansion, showing how model failure pinpoints unmodeled physical forces and missing resource constraints.
