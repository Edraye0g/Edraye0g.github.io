We deal with people everyday and we have to sustain certain type of patience along with it. This patience comes with either from our internal belief or our societal pressure. How we actually holds our patience or approach people completely depends on how the mechanism of anger and disgust has been built inside us. A study from *Scientific Reports* *[Anger and disgust shape judgments of social sanctions across cultures, especially in high individual autonomy societies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10920647/)* experimented on 17,774 individuals across 56 different countries to find how social judgement builds upon particular disgusting behavior and how different parameters like autonomy shapes our desire to punish broken rules.

Let's say you are in a classroom trying to concentrate on the lecture. Your classmate's phone continuously vibrating beside your desk and it is getting on your nerves. This is making your angry. But you let it go. This is happening quite sometime for almost a week. You cannot say anything due to **something.** Our mind makes decision based on our subjective judgments. The whole deviant behavior which is not that extreme but annoying enough to be dealt with faces some sorts of social actions from other people. 

 1. Confrontation
 2. Ostracism 
 3. Gossip 

![](/assests/anger1.png)

**Confrontation**
Mostly people approach to certain actions and it is driven by anger. This challenges the person who violated the norm of the place. 

**Ostracism**
A type of social exclusion by avoiding the individual. It is driven by both disgust and anger. 

**Gossip**
This is a ambiguous behavior, linking both anger and disgust. This damages the reputation of the violator without direct contact. 

![](/assests/anger2.png)

Historically, the western studies heavily relied on **WeIRD** or Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich and Democratic populations. This bias was completely overwhelmed by the inclusion of 6 different continents and people over 30 different languages from 56 different countries to see how actually this sanction and emotion mechanism works. It showed **anger** as their undisputed driver of direct confrontation. Their were other parameters like sad, afraid, surprised and happy other than anger and disgust. 

![](/assests/anger4.png)

___

Emotional disgust exists everywhere, but the influence on final judgment depends heavily on culture. The cultural moderator is the individual autonomy, low autonomy which is the outward traditional institutions and traditions for behavioral guidance. On the other hand, high autonomy relies on own feeling to dictate what is appropriate. The study shows high autonomy has cultural lossesness and the final judgement is sometimes biased or subjective rather than consistent. On the contrary, low autonomy has a significant consistency while judging violator. They have moral obedience and religious faith which makes their societal norm intact for small sanctions. Higher valuation of individual autonomy is mostly associated with emotion playing the larger role.

![](/assests/anger3.png) 

Global data reveals a rising trend in autonomy values across time as societies develop and modernize. The finding shows that as global reliance on religious and traditional authorities fades out, we may be entering an era where social judgements and informal punishments are mostly individual emotional intuitions.
