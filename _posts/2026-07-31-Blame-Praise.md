In your high school basketball tournament, you were trailing with 2 points and the clock is ticking for the final whistle. You must win the match or your favorite cheer-leader will be going on a date with the center from the other team, leaving you thrashed mentally. One break arrived with a counter attack and you had the chance to score the 3 pointer or pass to your teammate for only 2 points. You stumbled and in this pivotal moment, you went for the 3 pointer and missed. You had your choice but the game is over now. Not only you lost your cheer-leader, your whole school starts *blaming* you for the chance you missed due to a *split second* decision regardless of the 30  baskets you put before. The attribution of responsibility where human holds a figure accountable for an action or role based on an outcome lies on praise and blame game later. According to attribution theory in modern psychology, human brain explain others behavior based on blaming and circumstanes. If a person had freedom of choice from many options, he is likely to get pinned down for his role on certain decisions. 

Human brain processes all the thing based on newton's third law. *"Every action has an equal and opposite reaction".* With that in mind, the whole praise vs blame processing happens in a particular way that eliminates impulse thoughts in human brain while making clear deduction and judgement. 

1. Causality- What was the chosen action which led to the outcome?
2. Intention- Was the decision deliberate or accidental?   
3. Control- Could the outcome be avoided or any thing influenced to develop the outcome?
4. Norms- Societal norms and beliefs has impact on the judgement. An ingroup (people coming from same situation or society) will have leverage while blamed by another ingroup member. 
5. Consequence- How much any choice affected the outcome? A severe outcome due to a choice has much more impact on human decision making.

Together, these things built the urgency to counter factual outcomes in decision center and pin blames on other. 

A research published in *Springer nature* based on Experimental Economics, [Blame and praise: responsibility attribution patterns in decision chains](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10683-024-09833-1) shows exactlythe pattern how human brain process responsibility attribution on a choice maker. The study hovers to a decision task to identify that from a group which choice actually has most impact on human judgment. It's a higher dimentional study method not only focuses on the judge but also how the choice maker actually deals with choice based on a particular outcome. 

### Decision Task

Research was done on groups of 10 individuals. 5 voters and 5 recipients respectively. There were payoffs of fair and unfair rules. If voters voted for fair and that is the eventual outcome, all the 10 members will be getting 5 points. The unfair outcome decided 9 points to voters and 1 to each recipients.  

![](/assests/blame1.png)

After the voting, one of the recipients were asked randomly to punish or reward. If rewarded, none of the voters was sanctioned. if punished then points will be deducted from 0-7 points according to the recipient decision to each of the voters individually. 

### Findings

While sanctioning individual voters, recipients were very specific about whom they targetted. In most cases, it was the pivotal decider (The one whose vote decided the ultimate outcome). The decision to sanction was objectively absent if the oucome was fair. This shows that, blame or criticism only comes if the outcome is negative. 

![](/assests/blame2.png)

There are also a group of recipients who choses little to no punishment or reward at all, even if they had options. 

This study shows voting delegation. Voters use strategy deliberately to be the pivotal voter for further punishment even if unfair was their true preference. 

![](/assests/blame5.png)

### Measurers 

The task is paired with eye-tracking for recipients to see which voters they focus on while sanctioning. On the other hand, response time analysis for voters to measure the internal conflict and difficulty of making potentially pivotal decisions.
