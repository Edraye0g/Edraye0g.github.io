Imagine, you are at an apparel store. This store can let you take items for free. You take some cloths and rush towards the trial room. You see a person coming out of a trial room totally inflated like they have eaten an elephant. You saw him wearing cloths on top of each other. He really took way too much cloths. There’s a person in the cashier, who will observe the whole situation. The person is really intimidating. He is judging the whole scenario, taking notes of what is happening elsewhere. You would like to do the same as the greedy man from before. So, you hop into the room, put on some cloths and came out. You see the cashier looking at you. All of a sudden, something happened and you go back inside the room and take off some. 
What really happened here? Human is a social animal. Hence, human care about the very social image of themselves. If there was a beautiful lady on the cashier, no man would act like this greedy in front of her. According to sociology, that’s called **impression management due to gender norms.** However, social image prevents him to lie or cheat due to the fear of judgement. Now think about a camera or a robot is there. It depends on the perspective of how would you react. Study from **Business and Economic Research Laboratory (BaER-Lab), Department of Management, Heinz-Nixdorf-Institute, Paderborn University, Paderborn, Germany** shows that, human to human interaction concerns a fear of being judge. If not that, there’s misrepresentation of judgement that can lead to a though or self-justification or sometimes dependence on human empathy. Meaning if someone is lying, overcoming the boundary of social image, that would be a fabrication of understanding that the judge will sympathize with him/her or he/she can trick the system. The study also showed how ambiguity changed the perspective of how human look into a matter. If the system is transparent, human do some risk assessment to an extent where things will be overlooked by the judge. According to the study, that’s partial truth. On the contrary, human to machine ambiguity created a sort of **que sera sera** condition. There is zero risk assessment which lead to the tendency to lie more and play the system. However, this ambiguous condition also points toward the non-neutral fact. It means no partial truth. Either complete honesty or full lie. 

## Experiment:
The experiment talked about two distant hypotheses. The deterrence and the loophole. Deterrence meaning the decreasing of the percentage of cheat due to the understanding that machine is flawless and being inevitable is close to zero. On the other hand, the loophole tells us a story of how human to human interaction is so important for driving our behavior. As machines have no social connection, lying meant zero social image cost, shame or guilt.

The experiment was done with the **mechanics of deception**. The choice and verification test. A number was taken from a urn of 100 cards from 1 to 6. There was 95 percent of number 2 and the rest is the remaining number. It shows clear probablity of a drawn card being 2. The card was reported into a computer. The formula says that the reward/payout is reported number * 15 euros. However, there's a catch. There will be verification. Another lottery will be done choosing number from 1 to 10. If the drawn number is greater than the reported number, card will not be checked. Direct payoff. If the card is equal or smaller than the reported number, the actual card will be checked. But, it is not the end. If the card is equal to the reported number, direct payoff. otherwise, there will be another lottery. A new number will be drawn from the lottery between 1 to the reported number. If the number is equal or less, direct payoff. If not, there will be reduced payoff.

The experiment contains 4 categories of testing on the basis of transparency vs entity.
_ | **Human** | **Machine**
**Transparent** | Participants see exact audit probabilities and verified by human | participants see exact probabilities but verified by an algorithm 
**Ambiguous** | Participants are only told a human will make decisions 1 and 2 Probabilities are hidden. | Verified by algorithm but hidden probabilities.
