Imagine, you are at an apparel store. This store can let you take items for free. You take some cloths and rush towards the trial room. You see a person coming out of a trial room totally inflated like they have eaten an elephant. You see him wearing cloths on top of each other. He really took way too much cloths. There’s a person in the cashier, who will observe the whole situation. The person is really intimidating. He is judging the whole scenario, taking notes of what is happening elsewhere. You would like to do the same as the greedy man from before. So, you hop into the room, put on some cloths and came out. You see the cashier looking at you. All of a sudden, something happened and you go back inside the room and take off some. 
What really happened here? Human is a social animal. Hence, human care about the very social image of themselves. If there was a beautiful lady on the cashier, no man would act like this greedy in front of her. According to sociology, that’s called **impression management due to gender norms.** However, social image prevents him to lie or cheat due to the fear of judgement. Now think about a camera or a robot is there. It depends on the perspective of how would one react. Study from **Business and Economic Research Laboratory (BaER-Lab), Department of Management, Heinz-Nixdorf-Institute, Paderborn University, Paderborn, Germany**[Read here](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-economics/articles/10.3389/frbhe.2025.1645749/full)shows that, human to human interaction concerns a fear of being judged. If not that, there’s misrepresentation of judgement that can lead to self-justification or sometimes dependence on human empathy. Meaning if someone is lying, overcoming the boundary of social image, that would be a fabrication of understanding that the judge will sympathize with him/her or he/she can trick the system. The study also showed how ambiguity changed the perspective of how a human looks into a matter. If the system is transparent, the human does some risk assessment to an extent where things will be overlooked by the judge. According to the study, that’s partial truth. On the contrary, human to machine ambiguity created a sort of **que sera sera** condition. There is zero risk assessment which lead to the tendency to lie more and play the system. However, this ambiguous condition also points toward the non-neutral fact. It means no partial truth. Either complete honesty or full lie. 

## Experiment:
The experiment talked about two distant hypotheses. The deterrence and the loophole. Deterrence meaning the decreasing of the percentage of cheat due to the understanding that machine is flawless and being inevitable is close to zero. On the other hand, the loophole tells us a story of how human to human interaction is so important for driving our behavior. As machines have no social connection, lying meant zero social image cost, shame or guilt.

The experiment was done with the **mechanics of deception**. The choice and verification test. A number was taken from a urn of 100 cards from 1 to 6. There was 95 percent of number 2 and the rest is the remaining number. It shows clear probablity of a drawn card being 2. The card was reported into a computer. The formula says that the reward/payout is reported number * 15 euros. However, there's a catch. There will be verification. Another lottery will be done choosing number from 1 to 10. If the drawn number is greater than the reported number, card will not be checked. Direct payoff. If the card is equal or smaller than the reported number, the actual card will be checked. But, it is not the end. If the card is equal to the reported number, direct payoff. otherwise, there will be another lottery. A new number will be drawn from the lottery between 1 to the reported number. If the number is equal or less, direct payoff. If not, there will be reduced payoff.

The experiment contains 4 categories of testing on the basis of transparency vs entity.

_ | **Human** | **Machine** 
**Transparent** | Participants see exact audit probabilities and verified by human | participants see exact probabilities but verified by an algorithm 
**Ambiguous** | Participants are only told a human will make decisions 1 and 2 Probabilities are hidden | Verified by algorithm but hidden probabilities 


## Findings
Entity doesn’t matter when rules were clear. Across all four groups, there were roughly 40-60% of participants engaged in overreporting. But under transparent condition, average magnitude of cheating doesn’t differ between human and machine. *Verdict: When participants knew about the conditions and the probabilities, they quickly assessed the chances of how they could get the best out of the system.* 
But when there was ambiguity, during human auditor the chances of overreporting hasn’t significantly changed. That could happen due to social interaction theory. But algorithm, there was massive overreporting. *Verdict: Behavior radically polarized. Either participants stayed honest or lie to the absolute maximum.* 
**What will be, will be**
Why did that happen? Zero Social Image Cost. When the Algorithm is hidden, humans cannot calculate risk. They perceive the machine as highly error prone. Which destroys the logic of partial cheating completely. The very tools institutions deploy to catch fraud can backfire due to ambiguity. This can encourage extreme forms of fraud if their mechanism are kept hidden. 

## My thoughts
It is possible for institutions like tax/business agencies using human emotions to create vague sense of security. With more ambiguity, they can rationalize the condition to catch frauds and label them. If they can use ambiguity as a shield to solidify their presence in the human society which is technically unjustified, then what else can be achieved like advanced technology to create and manipulate human minds. Even transparency itself can cause situations like partial truth or lie which is more negative. People can justify their deeds with the understanding of transparency. Both are eventually confining us to a paradox.
