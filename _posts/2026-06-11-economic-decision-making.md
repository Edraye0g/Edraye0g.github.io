Let’s talk about game where the mechanics is to split a $10 bill between two, **a Proposer** and **a Responder.** 
Proposer suggests the split and the responder chooses to accept or reject. 
If accepted, the money is split between two. 
Rejection means no split, also no financial earn for any of the two. 

![](/assests/econo0.png)

This is called the **Ultimatum Game.**

## Theory of Utility 
According to the *Theory of Utility* from standard economic model, the proposer should act like a rational actor. He/she should consider the possibility of the highest gain which is also the rational solution for the proposer. Splitting $10 into $9:$1 is the most rational choice. 

![](/assests/econo1.png)


## Behavioral Economics 
However, behavioral economics adds few factors. In the brain of the responder, few things come up while making the choice. The psychology and emotion play a very conflicting game with the cognitive process of the brain. The standard model is challenged by the emotional factors. And the result is being unfair to the responder 20 or less than 20 percent triggers the responder to avoid/punish the unfairness. This shows the willingness to sacrifice financial gains in response to unfair offers. 
Social reputation plays significant role while making the choice. Reputation is an adaptive mechanism in human brain. This creates awareness to oneself about unfairness. Unfair proposal can also trigger emotional disgust which is conceptually similar to physical disgust. 

## Isolating Social Variable
To find the neural culprit behind the choices, researchers placed players inside fMRI scanner for 30 rounds of the game. Crucially, participants faced both human and computer partners proposing the exact same predetermined splits from $5:$5 to $9:$1 

![](/assests/econo2.png)


When computer program splits money for the two, even if there are unfairness the responder tends to accept it thinking that’s pure math. While being understanding, an unfair split from the human splitter triggers response to rejection. The response is not any mathematical error but a emotional reaction to social insult. 

## Conflict – Emotion vs Cognition

![](/assests/econo3.png)


The emotional center or the Anterior Insula evaluates the negative emotional states. It consistently fires in response to pain, hunger and foul odor. It treats insulting unfair offer as the same way as it treats a bad smell. It is the biological center of moral disgust. The more the unfairness, the higher the AIn activity. 
The Dorsolateral Prefrontal cortex is linked to executive control and maintains active cognitive goal. In the ultimatum game, the sole objective of this region is purely utility – accumulate as much money as possible. 

![](/assests/econo4.png)


With the AIn screaming **Reject!** and DLPFC demanding **Accept!**, the Anterior Cingulate Cortex activates. The ACC is brain’s conflict detecting monitor. Its heightened activity proves that decision-making is not a single calculation but a collision of opposing psychological drives. The choice is dependent on the out-weighting capability of the two distinct regions.  

## Decision

![](/assests/econo5.png)


Economic models can no longer treat emotion as a noise to be ignored. Sense of fairness is fine quantifiable and biologically hardwired variable.
