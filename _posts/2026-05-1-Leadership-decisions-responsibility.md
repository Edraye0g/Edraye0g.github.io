Imagine you are given a choice to make decisions for the institutional ceremony of your school. You are given a specific amount of money from the school board to cover the necessary financial burden. A team of 10 individuals you can select to help you out through out the arrangement. What you must do is to arrange the ceremony orderly, choose foods, select guests, select catering services, sending mails to people, approach faculty members and assistants. Seems easier right? While going through all that, you face some problems. To choose food, you must put other’s choice first. While selecting guests, you should check the background of the people you want to invite. To send mails of invitation to faculty members and assistant, you need to choose specific type of words rather than just babble unnecessarily. And, the most important is the team selection. You cannot just select anyone. They need to have sense of accountability and integration among themselves so that there is no mess. 
In every step, there’s a series of burden and responsibility. Your choice is to take the leadership or pass it on. Now, the question is **What will you do?**

Human brain processes subjective value in different ways. While making decisions, the mathematical framework relies heavily on the Bayesian theory. The prior belief and the posterior belief. Subjective value depends heavily on the difference between two significant conditions of whether a person will commit or let it pass to someone else. Here, comes the **prospect theory** where in peoples mind the probability of loss and significantly more effect than the probability of gain. If both the chances are same, the chance of loss has more effect on the chance of gain. It relies on certainty and demand. High demand and low certainty mean it will burn out all of energy to gain if there’s no luck factor involved. Vice versa, low demand and high certainty mean peace. This is where a wonderful study comes to talk about few hypotheses of how neurobiological foundation states leadership decision. The paper *Computational Neurobiological Foundation of Leadership Decisions* talks about how leadership decisions is connected with responsibility aversion. 
If we step back and see the bigger picture, few more questions might arise. Leadership is more about control and thrill seeking. It is about being easily let go any type of loss and taking risks more often. But, why responsibility aversion? 
To find the answers, researchers have gone through a set of experiments to find the correlation between how leadership score connects with responsibility aversion and other behavioral traits. 

## Experiment
The experiment consists of 3 phases,
-	**Group Induction phase:** Participants were socializing and bonding with each other prior any experiment was conducted over them. This helped the experiment further in the group delegation phase where there was risk of losing payoff as a group and the decision was to take a decision as a group for the study.
-	**Choice phase:** Participants were asked to gamble on a probability pie. The red slice showed the chances of loss, the green slice showed chances of gain. To make it more ambiguous, there was also a grey slice which meant this could be anything between gain and loss. They were given two choices. Whether to act or not act. 
-	**Delegation phase:** After two days, the individuals were called again. This time they were asked to work as a group of 4 including him. A new choice was added. Act, Not Act and *Defer.* There was informational advantage in the group. The members were shown different version of the same pie. This means two persons together has a more strategic winning percentage than one. The individual with the power of choice has the power to act or not act and defer. Defer means to pass it to the group for decision making.

The goal of the experiment was to find correlation between different traits of behavior, how individuals defer, how certainty and subjective value has effect over decision making, how responsibility of a group generates high certainty demand (psychological cost) while making decision and most importantly how the neurobiology works in this case?

# Data Collection
The participants were divided into two groups. 40 to behavioral group and another 44 to fMRI group. With the behavioral group, the behavior of choice was analyzed to develop a computational model. On the other hand, the fMRI group faced same task of choice but undergoing through a fMRI scan for BOLD.

# Analysis
The fMRI data used for brain region identification which help us to make decision making in two different levels of meta-cognitive activity. The 1st order evaluated the subjective value of two possibilities. 2nd order evaluation demands for certainty. Think of it like, buying a painting with $3000 to gift to someone as a student. 1st order cognition generates two subjective values of loss and gains from it. If the subjective value is wider between loss and gain, there will be effect of act and not act. If the value difference is less, you will ask the painter to whether the painting is worth the price. This is how subjective value helps us to make decisions. The decision is the actual 2nd order meta-cognitive process. 
Using Stochastic Dynamic Causal Modeling, a crucial question was answered and that is how information exchanged between different regions in the brain. On the experiment, different regions of brain evaluation to a certainty evaluates whether to act or to defer. **mPFC** or Medial Prefrontal Cortex is where the subjective value is actually calculated. The probability of leading/deferring is on **AIn** or Anterior Insula. But two other regions worked as an influence on the decision-making process. One is **TPJ** or temporoparietal junction which is essential for checking on the advantages specially on the social interactionism. As the group together has more advantage on choice making rather than one single person, this region actively participated prior to choices. Other one is the **TG** or temporal gyrus. This evaluated a prior understanding of the choice. Think of it like, if my gamble is wrong the effect will be on my teammates. Researchers found that, TG activity is lesser while choosing for himself vs choosing for the group. This shows that, human being is cautious and more calculative while taking responsibility of others.

**Deferral threshold:** It means when the defer choice will be taken by an individual. It is shown that when the stakes are high. Participants leaned towards more aversion of responsibility. If only the chances of gain or the certainty is higher, then they act based on their own judgement. The researchers came to a conclusion that, when there is other’s stake, participants create higher demand for certainty compared to self. This shows how unchanged subjective value generates different threshold values for self vs group. 


This model was used to predict whether the evaluation has capacity for out-of-sample real-life manifestation. Information was taken using a set of questionnaires and the role individuals are playing in real-life. The result was fascinating. Correlation between predicted and observed shift in deferral threshold based on neural activity was 0.79. correlation between predicted and observed leadership scores for the fMRI sample when using the combined neural and behavioral model was 0.47. Correlation between predicted vs observed leadership scores for fMRI sample when using only parameters estimated from the original behavior-only sample was 0.44. This shows the measures of responsibility aversion identified in the lab are robust predictors of both cognitive shift people experience under responsibility and their real-world leadership potential.
